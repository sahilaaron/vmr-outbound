{# Sequence rendering, shared by the Review queue and the Contact page. The rule that shapes every macro here: a collapsed card carries no message body, and an expanded card carries exactly one. Seven bodies per contact would make a Review queue of forty contacts unreadable and slow, and neither cost buys anything -- an operator reads one message at a time. Every value shown is escaped by Jinja's default autoescaping, exactly as the single-draft card is. Nothing here uses `| safe`. Values that can carry imported spreadsheet text -- the contact's name, the company's name, the campaign's name -- additionally pass through `neutralize`. Escaping stops them being read as HTML; `neutralize` stops them being read as a formula when an operator copies a cell out of this page and into a spreadsheet. See `app/services/imports/display.py`. **The message subject and body deliberately do not.** They are not imported cells; they are the email. An apostrophe injected in front of a body that legitimately opens with "-" or "+" corrupts the message an operator is about to read, copy and send, and the edit form used to prefill the neutralized text -- so opening the editor and saving persisted the corruption into the next version. Neutralization is a projection concern, and the projection that needs it is CSV, where a cell is evaluated. An email is not a cell.
